<p>Syllabus</p>
<p><b>GOLDICH v. DYLUS, GOD EMPEROR COMMISSIONER</b><br>ON WRIT OF CERTIORARI BEFORE JUDGMENT TO THE UNITED STATES FANTASY FOOTBALL COURT OF APPEALS<br>No. 26-1. Argued September 6, 2026. Decided September 7, 2026.</p>
<p>Petitioner Marc Goldich moved the Commissioner to move the League Draft from its scheduled Tuesday slot to 10:00 p.m. so that he and one other member could attend Back-to-School Night. The Commissioner, sitting as Presiding Justice of a court of his own designation, denied the motion under the standard of his "sole and gloriously arbitrary discretion," stating his complete reasoning as "I don't want to." Petitioner appealed to a court he created for the purpose. The Commissioner declined to read the appeal and referred it to the Office of the Clerk, a trash bin. Petitioner moved to compel and sought certiorari before judgment. This Court granted the petition and expedited the case.</p>
<p><i>Held:</i></p>
<p>1. A referral to an officer that cannot read does not terminate an appeal. The Phantom Clerk Doctrine is rejected. <i>Schrodinger v. Dylus</i>, 3 F.F. 1 (2026), reaffirmed. Pp. 2 to 3.</p>
<p>2. The Court of Appeals had jurisdiction. A tribunal created after the ruling it reviews is irregular, but the alternative proposed by the Commissioner is that he review himself, which no court has permitted since the League was founded and which this Court declines to be the first to permit. The principle of "too late" is adopted as the rule of decision. P. 3.</p>
<p>3. The order below is vacated. The Commissioner's standard contains a limiting term. His discretion must be not merely arbitrary but gloriously so, and "I don't want to" is a sentence any child can say. Reviewed for abuse of discretion, an order that states no reason states no discretion, and an order that states no discretion cannot have exercised it. Pp. 3 to 4.</p>
<p>4. Rule 23 permits a class of two where the two are the only members who have children at the school in question, and the class is certified as "parents who occasionally pretend to prioritize their children over fantasy football." The Court expresses no view on whether the pretense is sincere. P. 4.</p>
<p>5. The Draft shall commence at 10:00 p.m. on September 8, 2026. The Commissioner's further order that members attend "in good spirits" is beyond the power of any court and is vacated as well. P. 5.</p>
<p>Vacated and remanded.</p>
<p>PER CURIAM. DAVID, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment. One Justice dissented.</p>

## Per curiam

This is the Court's first case, and the Commissioner has helpfully arranged for it to present every question a court of last resort could want: whether it exists, whether it can hear anything, what standard it applies, and whether the parties will obey. We take them in that order, since the Commissioner has taken none of them in any order.

### I

The Commissioner did not read the appeal. He referred it to the Office of the Clerk, which the record establishes is a trash bin. He then argued, through silence, that a filing so referred is no longer pending. We call this the Phantom Clerk Doctrine because petitioner did, and because the Commissioner has offered no name of his own.

The doctrine fails on its own terms. A clerk receives filings; it does not dispose of them. The Office to which the Commissioner referred this appeal is capable of receiving and incapable of anything else. Petitioner's filing was therefore received and never acted upon, which is the definition of pending. *Schrodinger v. Dylus*, 3 F.F. 1 (2026), held that an appeal "remains pending whether or not the Commissioner opens the PDF." We reaffirm it. An appeal that is thrown away is still an appeal. It is merely an appeal in a bin.

### II

The Commissioner's jurisdictional argument, reconstructed from a three-word opinion and a one-sentence referral, is that his authority is "plenary and unreviewable," citing *The Commissioner Says So*, 1 F.F. 1 (perpetual), and that no appellate court existed when he ruled.

The second point is true and irrelevant. Courts are created by the people who need them, usually after the need arises. Petitioner needed one on the afternoon of September 2 and made one. The Commissioner's objection amounts to a claim that a party may not create a court to review him, from which it follows that the only court able to review him is the one he presides over, in which he is also the respondent. No member of this League would accept that arrangement in a trade dispute. We do not accept it in a scheduling one.

Petitioner styles the governing principle as "too late." The phrase is inelegant and correct. The Commissioner had every opportunity to establish a review mechanism before he needed one. He established a trash bin. Jurisdiction lies.

### III

We come to the merits, which are short, because the opinion below is.

The Commissioner announced his standard as whether he, "in his sole and gloriously arbitrary discretion, feels like granting the relief sought." Petitioner reads this as a confession. We read it as a rule, and we hold the Commissioner to it.

The standard has three elements. The discretion must be sole, and it is. It must be arbitrary, and "I don't want to" is nothing if not that. But it must also be glorious. The Commissioner chose that word. Words in a standard of review are not decoration. A gloriously arbitrary ruling is one that, however unreasoned, at least rises to the occasion: a ruling with style, with a flourish, with something a league member could quote at a bar. "I don't want to" is what a child says about a vegetable. It is arbitrary. It is not glorious. The Commissioner failed his own test.

Under ordinary abuse-of-discretion review the result is the same. A court that gives no reason has not exercised discretion; it has declined to. The order identifies no interest served by a 7:00 p.m. draft that a 10:00 p.m. draft would defeat, weighs nothing against petitioner's children, and disposes of the motion in the amount of time it takes to type nine characters. See *Goldich v. Dylus*, 2 F.F. 10 (2026) ("Come on, man."). That is an abuse.

### IV

Petitioner asks us to certify a class of two: himself and League member David, whose children attend the same school. The Commissioner does not oppose certification. He does not oppose anything, since he did not read the motion.

Rule 23 asks whether the class is so numerous that joinder is impracticable. Two is not numerous. But joinder of David was, on this record, impracticable, because David did not want to be joined to a filing this long. Commonality and typicality are satisfied: same school, same night, same Commissioner. Adequacy of representation is satisfied by the fact that petitioner has now filed three documents in one day. The class is certified as defined in the notice of appeal: parents who occasionally pretend to prioritize their children over fantasy football. Whether the pretense is sincere is a question of fact for the children, and it is not before us.

### V

The order below is vacated. The Draft shall commence at 10:00 p.m. on Tuesday, September 8, 2026.

One further matter. The order below directed that the Draft proceed "with all members in timely attendance and good spirits." Attendance a court can order. Spirits it cannot. That portion of the order is vacated for want of power, and the Commissioner is reminded that the same limit applies to him.

It is so ordered.

## David, J., concurring in the judgment

I join the Court's judgment. I write separately to note that I am a member of the certified class, that I did not recuse, and that nobody asked me to. The Commissioner's position throughout this case has been that no one may review him. I have no position on that. I have a Back-to-School Night at 7:00.

I would also decide this case on narrower grounds. The Court reaches the gloriousness of the Commissioner's discretion. I would hold only that the Commissioner did not read the appeal, and that a judge who has not read the appeal has not decided it. Everything after that is dicta, and I would prefer to be home for it.

## Dissent

One Justice dissents. The dissent was received by the Clerk. It has not been read. Under the Court's own holding today, it remains pending.
